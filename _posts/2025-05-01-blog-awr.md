In [previous blog](https://huazetang.github.io/posts/2025/how_it_works/kl_divergence/), we analyse the role of Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence in the reinforcement learning (RL). In this blog, we will answer to the question of weighted constraint and connect this method to advantage weighted regression (AWR), a famous offline RL algorithm.

Weighted log-likelihood ratio
======
Similarly, we adopt the settings:

* New policy (\\(\pi\\)): The policy of collecting data. 
* Old policy (\\(\pi_{\text{old}}\\)): The policy referred to (e.g. target policy, behavior policy or expert policy).
* Collected data (\\({\tau_i}_{i=1}^n\\) with \\(\tau = (s_i^1,a_i^1,\dots,s_i^{T_i}, a_i^{T_i})\\)): The data collected by new policy \\(\pi\\).
* Weighted function (\\(\omega(s,a)\\)): The weighted function for analyzing the value of state-action pair \\((s,a)\\).

Base on this settup, we have the weighted log-likelihood ratio as

$$
\text{WLLR} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{t=1}^{T_i}\omega(s_i^t,a_i^t)\log\frac{\pi(a_i^{t}\vert s_i^t)}{\pi_{\text{old}}(a_i^{t}\vert s_i^t)}.
$$

Therefore, we have that

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty} \text{WLLR} \overset{a.s.}{=} \mathbb{E}_{(s,a)\sim \rho_{\pi}} \left[w(s,a)\log\frac{\pi(a\vert s)}{\pi_{\text{old}}(a\vert s)}\right] =: D_{\text{KL}}^\omega\big(\pi(a\vert s)\| \pi_{\text{old}}(a\vert s)\big).
$$

Similarly, we adopt Monte Carlo method to approximate \\(D_{\text{KL}}^\omega\big(\pi(a\vert s)\| \pi_{\text{old}}(a\vert s)\big)\\) as

$$
\hat{D}_{\text{KL}}^\omega\big(\pi(a\vert s)\| \pi_{\text{old}}(a\vert s)\big) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \pi(a\vert s_i) \omega(s_i,a)\log\frac{\pi(a\vert s)}{\pi_{\text{old}}(a\vert s)}.
$$
